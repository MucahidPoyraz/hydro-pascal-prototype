Cylinder Sizing

Find the Right Cylinder with Our Cylinder Selection Software

18 JUN 2026

Hydraulic cylinder selection is not difficult for someone who knows the right formulas; but doing the calculation by hand every time is time-consuming and carries a risk of errors in unit conversion. HydroPascal's cylinder selection software was developed to remove both problems: you enter the values and the result and matching products appear.

Which Values Do You Need to Enter?

  • Pump pressure: The pressure the system works at. Found in the tractor's technical documentation or on the hydraulic label.
  • Pump flow: The amount of oil delivered per unit time. The value that determines speed of movement.
  • Cylinder bore: The main dimension determining the force produced.
  • Piston rod diameter: Affects retraction force and buckling resistance.
  • Stroke length: The travel distance.
  • Force unit: You choose whether the result is shown in kilograms or kilonewtons.

If some of these values are unknown, measuring the existing cylinder is often enough. The bore cannot be measured directly if the cylinder is not dismantled; in that case you go by the manufacturer's label or model information.

How to Interpret the Output

The software gives two kinds of result. The first is the calculation result: the force the cylinder will produce with the values you entered. The point to note here is that the extend and retract directions give different forces; as rod diameter grows, the force in the retract direction falls.

The second result is the matching products. HPL products that fit the dimensions you entered are listed. This list is a starting point; topics such as mounting type and installation dimensions must be checked separately on the product page.

When reading the result it helps to ask: how far above the force I actually need is the calculated force? The difference is your safety margin, and dynamic loads, sloping ground and system efficiency that falls over time are covered within that margin.

Advantages over Manual Calculation

The software's clearest advantage is not speed but consistency. The most common error in hand calculations is unit confusion: when bar and millimetres get mixed up with other units, the result can be off by a factor of a thousand, and sometimes nobody notices.

The second advantage is how easy it is to try scenarios. Increasing the bore by one step and seeing how the result changes takes seconds. This makes it easier to choose a balanced option in terms of both force and speed.

The third is matching the result directly with the product range. When a calculation result remains just a number, you still have to search for the product that corresponds to it; the software combines this step too.

Where Do You Find the Values?

The most common obstacle to using the software is not knowing the values to enter. There is a practical source for each.

  • Pump pressure and flow: Found in the hydraulic system section of the tractor or machine operator manual. On some models they are also on the pump label.
  • Bore: Measured directly if the cylinder is dismantled; otherwise found from the manufacturer's label or product code.
  • Rod diameter: Can be measured directly with a caliper while the cylinder is extended.
  • Stroke: Take two measurements with the cylinder fully closed and fully open; the difference is the stroke.

Even with only two of these four values in hand, you can try a reasonable range for the rest and see how the result changes.

The Tool’s Limits

The software is a pre-selection tool and does not replace detailed engineering calculation. Topics such as buckling checks, verification of mounting geometry and dynamic load analysis require application-specific assessment. In critical or unusual applications, the right approach is to treat the result as a confirmation step.

The result assumes your system works under ideal conditions. In reality, because of hose losses, valve pressure drops and pump efficiency that decreases over time, the force achieved in the field is somewhat below the calculated value. So it is right to read the result as a ceiling value.

Another limit of the tool is that product matching is dimension-based. The listed products fit the calculated values, but topics such as mounting type, installation distance and working environment should be assessed separately on the product page.
